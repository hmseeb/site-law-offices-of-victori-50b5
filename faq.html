Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about contacting the office, what to expect, and what this website can and cannot tell you. Anything not covered here, call (310) 488-6357.

Getting in touch

Reaching the office

How do I reach the office?

Call (310) 488-6357 — that is the fastest route. You can also email vclemansesquire@gmail.com or send the form on the contact page. The office is at 401 Wilshire Blvd PH, Santa Monica, CA 90401.

What happens after I send the contact form?

Your message goes directly to the office. Someone reviews it and follows up using the phone number or email you provided, so that you can talk through the matter properly. If your situation is urgent — an imminent court date, an active investigation, a deadline — call instead of waiting for an email reply.

Which areas does the office serve?

The practice is based in Santa Monica, California, and serves clients across the surrounding Los Angeles area. If you are unsure whether your matter is in range, call and ask — it is a short conversation.

Is it better to call or to write?

Call if anything is time-sensitive. Write if you would rather set out the background in your own words first, or if you are contacting the office outside business hours. Either way, keep the first message brief — detail is better shared once we have spoken.

Your case

About the work itself

What kinds of cases does Victoria Clemans handle?

Criminal defense, trial advocacy and litigation, and negotiation or conflict resolution. That includes complex matters where expert evidence — such as forensic audio and video analysis — is part of the case. See the practice areas page for detail on each.

What should I have ready when I call?

Whatever you already have: the charge or claim, any court date or deadline, the agencies or people involved, and any paperwork you were handed. If you have none of it, call anyway and describe what happened in your own words.

I have been accused of something I did not do. Is that the kind of case you take?

Yes — defending people against accusations, including false ones, is central to this practice. A colleague who has worked with the office as a forensic audio and video expert writes that Ms. Clemans “has been very successful with getting clients falsely accused of crimes, exonerated.” Every case turns on its own facts and evidence, so the first step is a conversation about yours.

Does my case have to go to trial?

Not necessarily. Many matters are resolved through negotiation rather than a verdict, and the two are prepared together — the strongest position in a negotiation is usually a case that is also ready for court. Which route makes sense depends on the facts and on what you want to achieve.

Can you promise a particular outcome?

No, and no attorney legitimately can. Results depend on the facts, the evidence and the court. What the office can give you is a clear explanation of the process, what can be challenged, and the realistic options in front of you.

Before you write to us

Confidentiality and this website

Does contacting the office create an attorney-client relationship?

No. Sending a form, emailing or leaving a voicemail does not by itself create an attorney-client relationship. A relationship begins only once the office confirms that it is representing you. Until then, please share only enough information for us to understand the matter.

Is the information on this website legal advice?

No. Everything here is general information about the practice. Advice depends entirely on the facts of your situation, which is why the first step is a conversation.

What information should I not put in a first message?

Avoid detailed admissions, confidential documents or sensitive third-party information in an initial, unsecured message. A short description of the type of matter, the stage it is at, and any deadline is enough to get the conversation started.

Practice area 01

Criminal defense

Outcome first: you stop guessing what the accusation means and start answering it with a plan.

Being investigated or charged is disorienting, and the first decisions often matter most — what you say, what you sign, which deadline is coming. This office represents people accused of crimes from early contact with investigators through hearings, motions and trial.

Where a case turns on recordings, timelines or technical material, outside specialists are brought in. One reviewing colleague describes working with the office “on several complex cases as a forensic audio and video expert” and notes success “getting clients falsely accused of crimes, exonerated.” Evidence gets tested rather than assumed.

What this looks like in practice

  • Reviewing the charge, the report and the evidence actually produced
  • Identifying what can be challenged, suppressed or explained
  • Coordinating expert review of audio, video and technical evidence
  • Appearing at hearings and arguing motions
  • Preparing for trial while keeping resolution options open

Practice area 02

Trial advocacy and litigation

Outcome first: your side of the story reaches the judge or jury clearly, in the order that makes sense to them.

A trial is won in preparation and in the moments that cannot be prepared for. The work includes building the narrative of the defense, pinning down the facts that matter, cross-examining the witnesses who carry the other side’s case, and arguing the law that frames all of it.

A review from a colleague describes it this way: “Her split second timing and knowledge of the law make her a great communicator and advocate for clients facing the judicial matrix.” That combination — preparation plus responsiveness in the room — is what courtroom advocacy asks for.

  1. Before

    Case theory and preparation

    The facts, the exhibits and the witness list are organised around a single clear theory of the case.

  2. During

    Examination and argument

    Direct and cross-examination, objections, and legal argument made as the hearing or trial develops.

  3. After

    Next steps

    What the ruling means, what follows it, and what options remain open to you.

Practice area 03

Negotiation and conflict resolution

Outcome first: a dispute resolved on terms you understand, without a fight you do not need.

  1. Negotiated resolutions

    Most matters are resolved by agreement rather than verdict. Negotiation is approached with the trial file already built — the strongest position at the table is the one that is also ready for court.

  2. Conflict management

    A reviewer notes that “Ms. Clemans’ history in criminal defense and conflict management makes her a natural negotiator in trial and conflict resolution.” That experience is applied to disputes that need de-escalation as much as argument.
